# Board cleanup

One pass that leaves the agent boards smaller and every open row at the row standard. It runs the cleanup tools that already exist, in order, over every board. Its in-flight, baseline, re-measure, report and next-pass steps follow `~/.local/src/docs/analysis-streams.md`; read it first. It lands no PRs, so the rank, lever, PR and proposal rules there do not apply. It writes no rows of its own: every edit is the `notes` CLI's, recorded in `~/.notes` git, so any one of them can be reverted.

The row standard is the notes-cli README, "The task row": `type(area): title` under 80 chars, one priority, `#size/` from `#pts/`, an epic (`#of/`), detail on indented lines. `notes doctor --projects` checks it. The board-size KPI is `board_days` in `fleet-bench room`, the board's weight in days of what the profile ships: how long the fleet takes to clear every planned row at its current rate. It has no limit, since the board is all planned work; a cleanup lowers it by dropping rows not worth doing or moving a nice-to-have to `improvements.md`.

## Scope and triggers

- The human says the board is full or asks for a board audit or cleanup.
- `notes doctor --projects` reports a board over its size budget or duplicate rows.

Not for a raw pile of new wants (`process-requests`), which rows dispatch runs next (`fleet-dispatch`), or the queue's depth and load (`fleet-pace`).

## Steps

1. **In flight first,** per the method, on both dotfiles repos: another session may already be cleaning a board. Note the `~/.notes` HEAD (`git -C ~/.notes rev-parse HEAD`) so step 5 can diff everything the pass wrote.
2. **Baseline.** Save these to the scratchpad:
   - `fleet-bench room` (board_days against its max, with board_open and backlog rows);
   - `notes doctor --projects` (findings by kind and project);
   - from `notes board --json`, per project, the open agent rows: total, `doing`, with no detail line, with no `#pts/`, untriaged, with no `#of/`, and older than 14 and 30 days (`.since`);
   - `agent-queue list --json --state active` by state. A `doing` row with a live queue item is work in the queue; count the `doing` rows with none separately, as the stale ones.
   - `fleet-bench chain`: `urgent_per_board` and `stale_urgent_d`, with the lines under the table that name the boards and the oldest row; and the share of queued items that are high, `agent-queue list --json --state queued | jq -s 'map(select(.priority == "high")) | length'` over its `length`.
3. **Standard fixes the doctor names.** Duplicate rows it prints get `notes ptask <p> [--profile bnb] --agent drop --dup <older id> -- <id>`, the id as the query (a `raw` copied from `notes board --json` matched no row on 2026-11-23, its case differed from the file's; a bnb project needs the profile flag before the `--`). A row whose children carry its work is a container, never a dup of its own child (2026-11-24: 44 parents were judged dups of their own split rows, ar-5374): judge a parent by its children, and leave it, since it closes itself when its last child closes. A real dup parent takes the same `drop --dup <kept id>`, which moves its open children under the kept row and refuses, naming the chain, a kept row that sits under it. `notes doctor --projects --untyped` proposes a typed row for each untyped one; apply the right ones with `ptask <p> --agent edit --was --text "<row>" -- "<old raw>"`.
4. **The backlog pass, board by board, biggest first:** `agentctl-inbox-triage --dry-run --backlog --project <p>`, then read its answers with `agentctl-inbox-triage --backlog --report` (every board's kept answers by verdict, no model call) or in `~/.local/state/agentctl/inbox-triage/backlog-<p>.jsonl` (every raise, improvement, done, dup, drop and move; a wrong one is deleted from the file, or set to `keep` with its `project` cleared, since a `project` alone still moves the row), then the same command without `--dry-run`, which writes only those reviewed answers. Before that apply, rewrite the answers file so a `dup`, `drop`, `done` or `improvement` answer for a `#requested` or `#urgent` row, or a `dup` of the row's own `#of/` parent (a child is no copy of its epic), becomes `keep`: `f=~/.local/state/agentctl/inbox-triage/backlog-<p>.jsonl; jq -c '. as $r | if ($r.action|IN("dup","drop","done","improvement")) and (($r.raw|test("#requested|#urgent")) or ($r.action=="dup" and ($r.raw|test("#of/"+$r.id+"( |$)")))) then .action="keep"|.project="" else . end' $f > $f.new && mv $f.new $f` (2026-11-23: 4 children and one household errand of the human's among 73; the apply's own guard covers a heading with children). It rewrites rows to the standard (the old title stays as a `was:` line), judges each row by the priority rubric (`~/.config/inbox-triage/rules/priority.md`) (raises a fault filed too low, sends an agent row that is only a nice-to-have to the project's `improvements.md` through `notes improve`, closes one that says it is done), drops dups and rows that are not tasks, and moves a row to the project it is about; a plan's finish row stays on its board and keeps its plan slug, since ask-sweep finds it by that slug there. It makes one Opus call per 40 rows on the inbox-backlog agent's own day budget (`~/.config/agentctl/agents/inbox-backlog.conf`), so it never spends the intake sweep's; a dry run stopped at that budget, or at the agent's hourly cap (`MAX_CALLS_HOUR` 30, about 1,200 rows), keeps the answers it got, and the next dry run, an hour on, asks only about the rest. Then, as needed: `--backlog --priority` (untriaged rows) and `--backlog --link` (rows with no epic), both on the inbox-priority agent's budget, and `--backlog --size` (rows with no points) on inbox-size's.
5. **Review what it wrote.** `git -C ~/.notes diff <step-1 HEAD> -- '*/agent/README.md'` and read every drop and dup. A wrong one is reopened with `notes ptask <p> --agent reopen <id>`, or edited back; say how many in the report. Then the parents the apply skipped: a parent is never a reason to keep a row. For each parent judged noise, and each container (a split row whose children carry all its work), judge every open child: drop the noise ones, deepest first; move each real one that has its own `goal:` or `check:` line to the parent's own epic (`notes ptask <p> --agent tag <child> --remove of/<parent> --add of/<epic>`, the epic open and on the same project), adding the parent's priority tag when the child's is lower; then drop the parent (`--why "split into <ids>"` for a container). The parent stays when any of these holds: it is `#requested`, `#urgent` or `#high` (the human's or the rubric's; list it for the human), it has no `#of/` epic, a child is `doing` (a live item: leave that child and the parent until it lands), or a real child has no brief of its own (the parent's lines are that brief, and it ranks as its children, adding nothing). The CLI refuses to close a row with an open child, so this order is the check (2026-11-24: 85 noise parents stayed open behind a rule that said never close one; 25 of them held the only brief of a real child).
6. **Rank.** Urgent says what starts first only while it is short: on each board `urgent_per_board` names past `urgent_per_board_max` (`~/.config/agentctl/kpi-thresholds`), and for each urgent row past `stale_urgent_d_max`, read the row against the rubric (`~/.config/inbox-triage/rules/priority.md`) and demote it (`notes ptask <p> --agent tag <id> --remove urgent --add high`), fold the copies of one cause into one row, close it with proof when it shipped, or keep it and say why in the report. A `#requested` urgent row is the human's: keep it, and list it for them. So is a row of a focus project's open sprint: notes-cli refuses a priority change on it, because an urgent row there holds the cut (`stages.md`, "Release windows", Frozen rows). List it for the human instead. Then `agentctl-inbox-triage --backlog --priority --retag high` re-judges every board's open `#high` rows by the same rubric (a started row keeps its tag: it ranks the live item), on the inbox-priority budget; review its tags as in step 5, and put back every `#requested` row it took below `#high` (before it runs, save their ids: `notes board --json | jq -r '.rows[]|select(.lane=="agent" and .state=="open" and .requested and .priority=="high")|.project+" "+.id' > ~/.cache/<session>/requested-high`; after, `notes ptask <p> --agent tag <id> --remove low --add high` for each id now lower; 2026-11-23: it lowered 23 of 204; the rubric files those high; tracked by ar-3010 until it lands, then delete this clause).
7. **Shipped rows.** `agent-land` closes the row its PR names, so a row a PR named is already closed (2026-11-13: 1 of 678 PR-named ids was still open, and it was a false match). A row delivered by a PR that never named it is found only by reading: for the oldest rows of each board, compare against `gh pr list --state merged --search "<key words>"`, and close one only when the PR delivers the whole row, `notes ptask <p> --agent done <id> --proof pr:<N>`.
8. **Quick wins.** Do not queue them by hand. Dispatch pulls eligible rows on its own (`fleet-dispatch`). Only the board fills the queue (`~/.config/agentctl/QUEUE.md`, "Admission"): an `agent-queue add` from this session writes no item, leaves or files its board row un-started and exits 5, so a hand add only repeats what dispatch does. A clean, typed, sized row with a detail line saying what done looks like is what makes a row eligible, so steps 3 to 6 are how the quick wins get picked.
9. **Re-measure and report** per the method: step 2 again, the numbers per project, what was dropped, merged, rewritten and moved (counts and a few examples), the wrong drops undone, and what is left: rows older than 30 days, rows with no detail. Old age alone never drops a row; list the oldest for the human instead.
10. **Queue the next pass** a week out every pass: the board refills weekly whatever "left" says, so this overrides the method's "left names a lever" condition and one for the boards a budget stop left, the next day (a row for the left boards when one is open already, e.g. a `board-cleanup step 4 backlog pass` row, covers it). File each as a board row, never `agent-queue add` (it refuses: "no board row to file this on"): `notes ptask agent-runtime --agent add "chore(notes): weekly board-cleanup pass per the board-cleanup skill; baseline: <this report> #of/<epic of the last weekly row> #pts/5 #size/m #at/<today +7>/1000 #low"`, after `notes board --json | jq -r '.rows[].text' | grep -qF "weekly board-cleanup pass"` finds no open row but this pass's own (the next-day row: today +1, and the title names the boards left).

## Do NOT

- Hand-edit a board file: every write goes through `notes ptask`, so the ingest record and git history stay true.
- Drop a row for being old or unclear. The human decides stale; the pass lists it.
- Close a `doing` row, or a parent before its children are dropped or moved (step 5): the first is a live queue item, the second leaves an orphan.
- Queue quick wins by hand while `fleet-bench room` reads full.
