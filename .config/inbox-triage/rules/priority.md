urgent: it breaks or blocks the person or production now, including a security hole or private-data exposure that is live in production now.
high: the project's next release needs it, a test or check is red on main (a red main holds every change's gate), the person asked for it (#requested), it blocks a focus project's epic or another agent's work, or it closes a hole that lets the wrong person in or out: an auth or authority check that is missing or trusted from the client, private data (PHI, PII, secrets, keys) readable by someone who should not see it, or a service reachable from the internet that should be private. Also high: a fault in the agent runtime (dispatch, the queue, sessions and shepherd, the boards and notes, telemetry and pages, CI gates) that gives a wrong state, a false page or nudge, or lost or stuck work: the person made agent-runtime stability the top priority, above game and feature work, until the runtime is stable (2026-11-24). Being filed under an open epic (#of/<epic id>) is not a reason: nearly every row is.
normal: real work with a named benefit (what gets better, for whom) that nothing waits on now: a feature, a fault that is not blocking anyone, epic work in its turn. This is the default for real work.
low: upkeep, cleanup or a check that may wait or be dropped: a tidy-up, a cleanup with a stated cost it removes, a re-measure, a chore such as a config setting on one more machine. A fault (fix) or a feature (feat) is never low.
Not a row at all: a nice-to-have, polish nobody asked for, a "could also", an idea with no named benefit. That is an improvement, and it is kept in the project's improvements.md, never at any priority.
When unsure between two priorities, pick the lower, but real work with a named benefit is never below normal, and neither is any fault or feature.
